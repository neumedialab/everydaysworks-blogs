# Berlin’s Answer: The Left Party Comes First as Elif Eralp Faces a Coalition Test

## What the 20 September result confirms

In Berlin’s 20 September 2026 election for the Abgeordnetenhaus, Die Linke came first in the preliminary results published by the Berlin election authority. The party received 25.7% of the second vote, ahead of the CDU at 18.8%, the AfD at 16.2%, the Greens at 14.3% and the SPD at 12.1%. The figures remain preliminary, so this article does not present a final seat distribution or a completed government as fact. [1]

## Who is Elif Eralp?

According to Deutsche Welle’s profile, Eralp was born in Munich in 1981 to a socialist and trade-union family that sought asylum in Germany after Turkey’s 1980 military coup. She studied law and worked for many years in legal policy. Her Berlin campaign connected political representation for people with migration backgrounds with housing and social justice. [3]

Die Linke coming first is not the same as Eralp being elected Governing Mayor of Berlin. The office is not chosen directly by voters; it is elected by the newly formed state parliament. Eralp would need coalition support and a successful parliamentary vote.

## Why this result matters

The AfD’s preliminary second-vote share in Berlin rose to 16.2%, a clear increase from 9.1% in 2023. Yet the AfD did not come first in Berlin, and the other major parties have ruled out coalition cooperation with it. [1] [2]

The result is therefore more than a party ranking. At a moment shaped by Trumpism, visible support for the far right from powerful technology-capital actors, and an anti-immigrant political wave, the rise of a lawyer from a migrant family as one of Berlin’s strongest political candidates can be read as an important democratic answer. This sentence is editorial interpretation, not a reported fact.

## Housing and the coalition test

Eralp and Die Linke built their campaign around high rents, the housing crisis and social justice. The proposal to move housing owned by large real-estate corporations into public ownership will remain one of the most contested issues in coalition negotiations. It is a political objective, not an implemented policy. [4]

Die Linke does not have a majority on its own, so a coalition with the SPD and the Greens is one possible route. The party’s electoral victory is clear; Eralp’s appointment to the office is not yet settled.

## Why this may matter to people in Turkey

This publication also considers the Berlin result from the perspective of people in Turkey who are concerned about political pressure, weakened rule of law and growing hopelessness. Berlin is not a direct political template for Turkey. It does, however, offer a powerful image of democratic participation, representation and organised political change remaining possible.
